## A digital notice board on the screen you already own

A cork board says whatever somebody last pinned to it. A **digital notice board** says whatever you published this morning, and it says it to everybody who walks past. The screen can be a TV in a corridor, a monitor at reception, or a small display by a door.

You do not need special hardware, and you do not need to install anything on the display. Everything on this site is a web page, so any screen that can open a URL can be your notice board: a smart TV, a cheap streaming stick, a Raspberry Pi, or a browser in kiosk mode. Pick what you want on it, paste the link into your player, and leave it.

## What goes on it

Most notice boards do a handful of jobs at once, so the pieces below are meant to be mixed. Put a clock and the weather beside your own announcements, or rotate through several screens on a playlist.

- **Your own words.** If you run a WordPress site, [Screenly Cast](/screenly-cast/) renders the posts and pages you already publish for a screen, so an announcement written once appears on the board as well as the web.
- **Live news and updates** from any [RSS or news feed](/rss/), which is how a board stays current without anybody touching it.
- **Practical detail** people actually stop to read: [opening hours](/opening-hours/), a [menu](/menu-board/), the [weather](/weather/) and [air quality](/air-quality/).
- **A clock**, in [one timezone](/clock/digital/) or [several](/world-clock/), which is the single most-looked-at thing on any wall.
- **Something worth pausing for**, like [this day in history](/on-this-day/), a [quote](/quotes/), or the [phase of the moon](/moon/).

## Notice boards for schools

A school corridor is the classic case for an **electronic notice board**: term dates, the lunch menu, sports results, and whatever the office needs everybody to know today. A screen beats paper because the person updating it does not have to walk anywhere, and because nobody has to notice that a new sheet went up.

Start with news feeds and opening hours, add a clock so pupils can see whether they are late, and use Screenly Cast if the school already runs a WordPress site.

## Notice boards for offices and reception

In an office the board is usually doing two jobs: telling visitors they are in the right place, and telling staff something they would otherwise get in an email nobody reads. Reception screens work well with the weather, a world clock for distributed teams, and your own announcements.

Because the content is just a web page, one screen can be a **digital bulletin board** for the whole floor and cost nothing beyond the display.

## Notice boards for churches and community spaces

Service times, this week's readings, the hall booking calendar, and a photograph. A church, village hall or community centre usually wants the board to look calm rather than busy, so one thing at a time on a slow rotation reads better than a dense dashboard.

## What it costs

Nothing, for the apps. Every screen on this site is free to use and free of accounts, and the links work on any player.

If you also want the software that drives the screen to be free and open-source, [Anthias](https://anthias.screenly.io/?utm_source=signage-apps.com&amp;utm_medium=referral&amp;utm_campaign=digital-notice-board) runs on a Raspberry Pi and costs nothing. If you would rather somebody else managed the fleet, [Screenly](https://www.screenly.io/) does that commercially. Both open a URL, and so does almost everything else, so nothing here locks you to either one.

## Start with one screen

Pick something from the library below, paste its link into your player, and see how it looks on the wall before you plan anything bigger. A board that shows one useful thing reliably beats a board that shows six and goes stale.
